Overview

The rules and numbers that matter — not the full lecture.

3 required properties

  • Not toxic — no harmful effect on the body
  • Not volatile
  • Stable — no interaction with the active ingredient; holds up in storage

Solubility reference

Solubilities of many medicinal agents are stated in the USP-NF (United States Pharmacopeia–National Formulary) and other reference books.

Chloroform + water

Dissolves in 200 volumes of water — about 200 mL of water is needed to dissolve 1 g. Not flammable, but its vapor decomposes near naked flames into harmful gases.

Glycerin needs heat

Glycerin must be heated to reduce its viscosity before it is used as a solvent — otherwise substances are difficult to dissolve in it.

Water purification, in order

Crude → Tap → Distilled/Deionized → Purified → Sterile → Pyrogen-free → Bacteriostatic (each step for injectable water adds one more requirement).

One replaces, one dissolves aspirin

Propylene glycol is used as a modern replacement for glycerin. Polyethylene glycol dissolves water-insoluble substances such as acetylsalicylic acid.

Not miscible

Glycerin is miscible with water and alcohol — but explicitly not with chloroform.
