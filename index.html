Pharmaceutical Technology I · Lecture 4

Study Smart

Solvents used in pharmaceutical preparation — the rules, the numbers, and the recall tools you need.

Overview

The rules and numbers that matter — not the full lecture.

1 · Core Idea

Pharmaceutical solvents are the vehicles used to dissolve drugs. Each has its own solubility and miscibility profile and its own best use. The lecture covers the 3 properties every pharmaceutical solvent must have, then 9 solvents: Water, Alcohol USP, Diluted Alcohol NF, Dehydrated Alcohol USP, Isopropyl alcohol, Chloroform, Glycerin, Propylene glycol and Polyethylene glycol.

2 · Learning Points

  • Solubilities of many medicinal agents in the usual solvents are stated in the USP-NF and other reference books.
  • A pharmaceutical solvent must be not toxic, not volatile, and stable.
  • Water is the main vehicle; the types of water differ by the processing steps applied.
  • Alcohol exists in four strengths/forms, and its content in oral products is limited by age because of its toxic effects, particularly in children.
  • Co-solvents (glycols, glycerin) are used with alcohol to reduce the amount of alcohol required.
  • Miscibility facts (what mixes with what) distinguish one solvent from another.

3 · Key Definitions

USP-NF

The United States Pharmacopeia National Formulary — the reference stating solubilities of many medicinal agents in the usual solvents.

Hydroalcoholic liquid

Alcohol–water mixture (Diluted Alcohol NF); dissolves both alcohol-soluble and water-soluble substances.

Dehydrated / absolute alcohol

Alcohol USP at 99.5% C2H5OH — practically free from water.

Volumes of water

Milliliters of water required to dissolve 1 g of a compound (e.g., chloroform: 200 volumes).

Miscibility

Ability of a solvent to mix with other solvents (e.g., water with alcohol and glycerin), making it a useful vehicle.

5 · Important Concepts

Water

Good solvent for most inorganic salts and many organic compounds. Its miscibility with alcohol and glycerin makes it a useful vehicle for many pharmaceutical preparations.

Alcohol USP

Solvent for certain drugs insoluble in water, such as organic substances (natural and synthetic). Dissolves plant constituents: resins, volatile oils, alkaloids, glycosides. Also an antimicrobial preservative, alone or as a co-preservative with others such as parabens.

Alcohol with co-solvents

Used with glycols and glycerin to reduce the amount of alcohol required, because alcohol has undesired pharmacologic and toxic effects when ingested in pharmaceutical products, particularly by children.

Diluted Alcohol NF (49–50%)

Prepared by mixing equal volumes of alcohol USP and Purified Water USP. Used in manufacture of certain preparations; useful in various processes since it dissolves both alcohol-soluble and water-soluble substances.

Dehydrated Alcohol USP (99.5%)

Practically free from water. Chief pharmacy use: research and analytic work, and preparation of synthetic organic medicinals.

Isopropyl alcohol (70%)

Used in cosmetic and dermatologic formulation.

Chloroform

Miscible with alcohol, ether, benzene, solvent hexane, and both fixed and volatile oils. Dissolves in 200 volumes of water. Not flammable, but its vapor decomposes by naked flames producing harmful gases. Solvent for many alkaloids.

Glycerin, USP (Glycerol)

Clear, syrupy liquid with a sweet taste. Miscible with water and alcohol, not with chloroform. Excellent solvent for tannins, phenol and boric acid. Preservative qualities — used as a stabilizer for solutions prepared with other solvents, and as an auxiliary solvent with water or alcohol. Used in many internal preparations. Must be heated to reduce viscosity when used as a solvent; otherwise it is difficult to dissolve substances in it.

Propylene glycol

Miscible with water, acetone, alcohol and chloroform. Dissolves many essential oils (volatile oils) but is immiscible with fixed oils. Wide range of usefulness as a solvent; used as a replacement for glycerin in modern pharmaceutical and cosmetic formulations.

Polyethylene glycol

Miscible with water, acetone, alcohol and other glycols. Dissolves many water-soluble organic compounds and certain water-insoluble substances such as acetyl salicylic acid.

6 · Important Differences

Alcohol forms compared

Diluted Alcohol NF, 49–50%Equal volumes of alcohol USP + Purified Water USP; dissolves alcohol-soluble and water-soluble substances
Isopropyl alcohol, 70%Cosmetic and dermatologic formulation
Alcohol USP, 94.9–96%Solvent for water-insoluble drugs and plant constituents; antimicrobial preservative
Dehydrated Alcohol USP, 99.5%Practically water-free; research, analytic work, synthetic organic medicinals

Glycerin vs Propylene glycol vs Polyethylene glycol

GlycerinMiscible with water and alcohol, not chloroform; needs heating (viscous); also a stabilizer
Propylene glycolMiscible with water, acetone, alcohol and chloroform; immiscible with fixed oils; replaces glycerin in modern formulations
Polyethylene glycolMiscible with water, acetone, alcohol and other glycols; dissolves acetyl salicylic acid

7 · Processes / Stages

Water purification pathway

  • Crude Water → Clarification → Tap Water
  • Tap Water → Distillation → Distilled Water
  • Tap Water → Deionization → Deionized Water
  • Distilled Water and Deionized Water → Purified Water (distilled & deionized)
  • Purified Water → Sterilization → Sterile Water (for injection)
  • Sterile Water → Removal of pyrogen → Pyrogen-free Water (for injection)
  • Pyrogen-free Water → Preservative addition → Bacteriostatic Water (for injection)

9 · Clinical / Practical Importance

Recommended alcohol limits (oral products)

OTC oral, children under 6 years0.5%
Children 6 to 12 years5%
Over 12 years and adults10%

Limits exist because alcohol has undesired pharmacologic and toxic effects when ingested, particularly by children.

Practical uses

  • Glycerin: used in many internal preparations
  • Isopropyl alcohol: cosmetic and dermatologic formulation
  • Propylene glycol: cosmetic and pharmaceutical formulations
  • Dehydrated alcohol: research and analytic work

Chloroform safety

Not flammable, but its vapor decomposes by naked flames producing harmful gases.

10 · Exam Focus

  • The 3 properties of a pharmaceutical solvent: not toxic, not volatile, stable.
  • The order of the water pathway and the step that creates each type of water.
  • The alcohol percentages: Diluted NF 49–50%, Isopropyl 70%, Alcohol USP 94.9–96%, Dehydrated USP 99.5%.
  • Diluted Alcohol NF = equal volumes of alcohol USP and Purified Water USP.
  • Alcohol limits: 0.5% / 5% / 10% for under 6 / 6–12 / over 12 and adults.
  • Chloroform: 200 volumes of water; solvent for alkaloids; vapor decomposes by naked flames.
  • Glycerin: miscible with water and alcohol but not chloroform; dissolves tannins, phenol, boric acid; heat to reduce viscosity.
  • Propylene glycol: miscible with chloroform; immiscible with fixed oils; replaces glycerin.
  • Polyethylene glycol dissolves acetyl salicylic acid.
